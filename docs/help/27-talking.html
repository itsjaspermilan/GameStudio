Talking: text boxes and choices

Two calls do conversations. gs.say(name, text) shows a text box, types the words out and waits until the player presses A. gs.ask(question, answers) asks a question with answers to pick with up and down, and gives back the number of the one picked. While a box is up the world stands still: nothing moves, nothing hits the player. The box opens out from its middle and folds shut at the end, like a GBA window; one gs.say straight after another keeps it open (gs.ui.style.open_time = 0 turns that off).

Both wait for the player, so they go inside a little script that can wait: gs.go(function() ... end) in a scene, self:go(...) in an entity (see Timers). Inside it the conversation reads from top to bottom, like a script for a play.

A conversation

local Village = gs.scene{}

function Village:enter()
  gs.go(function()
    gs.say("OLD MAN", "It's dangerous to go alone. The cave to the north is full of bats.")
    local pick = gs.ask("Will you take my lamp?", { "YES", "NO" }, "OLD MAN")
    if pick == 1 then
      gs.say("OLD MAN", "Good. Bring it back when you are done.")
      self.lamp = true
    else
      gs.say("OLD MAN", "Then be careful in the dark.")
    end
  end)
end

function Village:draw()
  gs.draw.rect(0, 0, 320, 240, "#14234f")
  gs.draw.text(self.lamp and "YOU HAVE A LAMP" or "A QUIET VILLAGE", 8, 8, "#ffffff")
end

return Village
return gs.game{
  title = "Village",
  console = "glow43",
  start = "scenes.village",
}

Long text wraps to the box and runs onto more pages; a blinking arrow says there is more. A press of A while it is still typing shows the whole page at once.

The box's look

Without anything else the box sits at the bottom: dark tall letters with a soft shadow on a pale page, like a GBA game. To give it your game's look, put these in the scene's layout (Layouts):

  • a panel named dialogue_box: where the box is, and the tiles it is made of;
  • a text named dialogue_text: where the words start, and their font, colour, size and shadow;
  • a text named dialogue_name (if you want the speaker's name in a place of its own, over the box for example);
  • a picture named dialogue_cursor: the arrow in front of the chosen answer and at the end of a page.

They are only a pattern: the game does not show them by itself, only when something is said. The words keep the same distance from the box's right and bottom edges as from its left and top.

Menus the same way

gs.ui.menu can take its look from the layout too: gs.ui.menu({ "PLAY", "QUIT" }, { at = self.menu_text, cursor = self.menu_cursor }) puts the list where that text is, in its font and colour, with that picture as the cursor. Then menu:update() in update and menu:draw() in draw_ui, as in Scenes, menus and saving.
