Sprite and tile sizes

Glow43 is 320×240 pixels: half as tall again as a Game Boy Advance (240×160). Art drawn at GBA sizes looks small on it. On a GBA, Link or Sonic is about 24 to 40 pixels tall, 15 to 25% of the screen; the same share of Glow43 is 32 to 48 pixels. These sizes give Glow43 games the same proportions, with room for GBA-style shading:

WhatSuggested sizeWhy
Hero or main character32×32, up to 48×4813 to 20% of the screen height: room for a face, shading on every part, and animation. New sprites start at 32×32.
Common enemy24×24 to 32×32Still readable when there are many, a little smaller than the hero.
Big enemy or mini-boss48×48 to 64×64Clearly bigger than the common ones.
Boss64×64 to 128×128128 is the limit: a boss can fill half the screen.
Bullet, pickup, particle8×8 (up to 16×16)Small but visible.
Tile16×16, or 32×32 for detailed placesTiles are 8×8, 16×16 or 32×32. Bigger tiles leave room for texture (woven floors, tufts of grass, bark) without repeating.

Text8×8 lettersThe built-in font. Text wants to be small.

The rules

  • Sprites are multiples of 8 in width and height, up to 128×128 on Glow43 and Glow169.
  • A sprite is a strip: all frames are the same size, side by side.
  • Frames in a sprite: up to 32 (a soft budget).
  • A tileset sheet is up to 512×512 (256 tiles of 32×32, or 1024 of 16×16).

Judging size

A 16×16 hero is 7% of the height of Glow43: a crowd of them reads as a swarm, but a lone hero looks small. The Handheld tab shows your biggest sprite against the limit. When unsure, draw the hero at 32×32 and an ordinary enemy at 24×24: the hero should be bigger than what it fights, and the player's eye finds it first.

Keep the screen at the console's size. A smaller screen is not recommended: the display is not an even multiple of it, and uneven pixels look worse than slightly small sprites.
