Sprite and tile sizes

Dot is 320×240 pixels, twice a Game Boy Advance in each direction that matters. Art drawn at GBA sizes looks small on it, and tiny sprites are hard to read on a small screen. These sizes feel right:

WhatSuggested sizeWhy
Hero or main character24×24 or 32×3210 to 13% of the screen height: room for a face and for animation.
Common enemy16×16 to 24×24Still readable when there are many.
Big enemy or mini-boss32×32 to 48×48Clearly bigger than the common ones.
Boss48×48 to 64×6464 is the limit.
Bullet, pickup, particle8×8 (up to 16×16)Small but visible.
Tile16×16 (32×32 is not allowed: use 16)Tiles are 8×8 or 16×16.
Icon16×16
Text8×8 lettersThe built-in font. Text wants to be small.

The rules

  • Sprites are multiples of 8 in width and height, up to 64×64 on Dot and Dash.
  • A sprite is a strip: all frames are the same size, side by side.
  • Frames in a sprite: up to 32 (a soft budget).
  • A tileset sheet is up to 256×256.

Judging size

A 16×16 hero is 7% of the height of Dot: a crowd of them reads as a swarm, but a lone hero looks small. The Handheld tab shows your biggest sprite against the limit. When unsure, draw the hero at 24×24 and an ordinary enemy at 16×16: the hero should be bigger than what it fights, and the player's eye finds it first.

Keep the screen at the console's size. A smaller screen is not recommended: the display is not an even multiple of it, and uneven pixels look worse than slightly small sprites.
