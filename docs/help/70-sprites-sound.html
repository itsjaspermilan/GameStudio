Sprites, animation and sound

Every game starts with rectangles and circles, and that is a fine way to make it work. When you are ready for art and sound, the editors in the studio write plain files into your game's folders and the engine finds them by name. Nothing has to be listed or registered anywhere.

A sprite

  1. In the list on the left, press + next to Sprites and type a name such as hero. The pixel editor opens with a blank 32x32 picture, a good start for a hero; Resize changes it (any multiple of 8 up to 128x128 on Glow43, for bosses). (Not sure about the size? See Sprite and tile sizes.)
  2. Draw. Pick the sprite's colour bank at the top left; its 15 colours and see-through are below it (Glow43 works like a GBA: each sprite uses one bank of the game's colours, see Drawing pixel art). Choosing another bank moves every pixel to the same place in the new one. Pixels outside the bank are flagged, and Snap to bank fixes them. A tileset ticks the tile banks it uses instead, and each tile keeps to one of them.
  3. To move pixels, take Select (S), drag a box around them, then drag inside the box. The arrow keys nudge them one pixel (Shift: eight), F flips them left to right, Delete clears them, and Esc puts them down. Cmd/Ctrl+C, X and V copy, cut and paste, also into another frame or another sprite (pasted pixels land where they were copied from). One Undo takes a whole move back.
  4. The frames are in the strip along the bottom of the editor. Add one with the + after the last frame, or Copy the one you are on (copying a frame and changing a little is the quick way to animate). Drag a frame to change the order. The preview plays them at the speed you set.
  5. Set the origin: the point of the picture that sits on the entity's position. For a character that walks on the ground, put it between the feet. For a ball, the middle.
  6. Leave the collision box alone, and the whole picture is the hitbox. Make it smaller (just the body, not the sword) for collisions that feel fair.

The files are sprites/hero.png (all the frames side by side) and sprites/hero.json. Now use it: Make an entity with it at the top right of the sprite editor makes entities/hero.lua for you, or put the sprite in an entity you already have:

local Hero = gs.entity{
  sprite = "hero",              -- sprites/hero.json
  tags = { "hero" },
}

An entity with a sprite is drawn automatically at its position, and its hitbox is the sprite's collision box. Entities off the edge of the screen are skipped, so they cost nothing.

Animations

A new sprite's frames are one animation that loops ("All frames" in the Animations list at the bottom left). For a hero that stands, runs and falls over, give it several:

  1. Press + next to Animations and name the frames you have, for example idle. They all go into it.
  2. Press + again for each of the others (run, die). Each starts with one blank frame; the + at the end of the strip adds more, and Copy copies the frame you are on into the same animation.
  3. Over the strip, give each a speed (fps) and say whether it loops. die should not: it stays on its last frame.

Click an animation in the list to see its frames; the preview plays it. Drag a frame onto another animation in the list to move it there. The first animation is what an entity shows until its code plays another; × deletes an animation with its frames (on the only one, it just takes the name off).

function Hero:update(dt)
  local dx = gs.input.dir()
  self.x = self.x + dx * 80 * dt
  if dx ~= 0 then
    self:play("run")             -- does nothing if "run" is already playing
    self.flip = dx < 0           -- mirror the picture to face left
  else
    self:play("idle")
  end
end

-- a clip that does not loop tells you when it reached its last frame
function Hero:on_animation_end(name)
  if name == "die" then self:destroy() end
end

Without animations the whole sprite loops at its speed. self.frame is the frame being shown and self.anim_speed speeds the animation up or slows it down (1 is normal). Other things every entity has for its looks: alpha (0 invisible to 1), sx and sy (scale, negative mirrors), angle (degrees), visible, and depth (bigger is further back). self:set_sprite("hero_hurt") switches to another picture. In code that is not an entity, gs.draw.sprite("star", x, y, { frame = 1, scale = 2 }) draws one.

Tilesets

A tileset is a sheet of small square pictures. In the tileset editor you set the tile size (8, 16 or 32 on Glow43; 32 is the usual size for ground and walls, 8 or 16 for window and panel edges), then draw tiles on the sheet. The tiles are numbered left to right, top to bottom. They are drawn into a map with a tile layer: see Big levels.

Sound effects

A sound effect is a short tune made of steps. In the sound effect editor, press + next to Sound effects, name it (jump), and drag an instrument onto a lane. Click in the lane to place notes; press play to hear it. It is rendered by the same synthesizer the game uses, so it sounds the same on the handheld.

gs.sfx("jump")          -- plays sfx/jump.json
gs.sfx("jump", 0.5)     -- at half volume
gs.sfx_stop()           -- stop all sound effects

Music

A song has eight tracks, each with an instrument (Glow43 and Glow169 play all eight, like a GBA game's music). Reverb at the top makes it sound as if played in a space: Room (short and warm), Hall (long and bright) or Cave (very long and dark), and the slider says how much. A little goes a long way: most GBA music had some. Sound effects are dry; give one a Reverb amount (a spell, a bell, a door in a hall) and it rings in the song's space. Songs are patterns of notes in a grid; the music editor can also suggest a melody, a drum beat or a chord to start from.

gs.music("title_theme")         -- starts music/title_theme.json, replacing what played
gs.music_stop(1.5)              -- fade out over one and a half seconds

Sound effects and music use separate voices, so a sound effect never cuts a note out of the song. The handheld's speaker is small: bright, short sounds carry better than deep ones.

Instruments and samples

Instruments are the sounds that songs and effects are played with. A set of ready-made ones (pulse waves, noise drums, a General MIDI piano and more) is always available. To change one, copy it into Instruments and edit the copy. You can also import a short .wav recording as a sample (up to 8 seconds on Glow43; it is made mono at 32 kHz, the best a GBA game used) and use it as an instrument.

Missing sounds are errors on purpose. gs.sfx("jmup") stops with "unknown sound effect 'jmup'" instead of staying silent, and the editor underlines it before you even run. Typos should not hide.
